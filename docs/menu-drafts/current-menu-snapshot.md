- **The Kaku-Hi:** Chilled Japanese whiskey and bubbly club soda with a bright lemon twist.
  *Story: Kakubin ("square bottle") — Suntory's 1937 blend by founder Shinjiro Torii, still Japan's best-selling whisky. Suntory's Torys bars made the whisky highball the default serve in the 1950s, and a late-2000s highball revival (ice-cold glass, 1:3–4 soda, single stir) turned it into Japan's everyday meal-time drink.*
- **Old Fashioned:** Bold bourbon lightly sweetened, stirred with fresh orange oils and aromatic bitters.
  *Story: the original cocktail. In 1806 a Hudson, NY newspaper defined "cock-tail" as spirits + sugar + water + bitters ("bittered sling"). When late-1800s bartenders started fancifying drinks, regulars asked for whiskey cocktails "the old-fashioned way." Louisville claims the name via the Pendennis Club (late 1880s, carried to the Waldorf-Astoria) and made it the city's official cocktail in 2015.*
- **Whiskey Sour:** A silky, frothy balance of robust bourbon, tart lime, and velvety egg white.
  *Story: first printed in 1862 in Jerry Thomas's Bar-Tenders Guide (America's first cocktail book). The sour family descends from sailors' citrus rations against scurvy. Egg white makes it a Boston Sour; a red-wine float makes it a New York Sour.*
- **Gold Rush:** Smooth whiskey blended with sweet honey and a sharp, citrusy squeeze of lime.
  *Story: modern classic, early 2000s, Milk & Honey NYC. T.J. Siegal (friend of owner Sasha Petraske) ordered a whiskey sour and swapped the sugar for honey syrup Petraske had on hand. Named on the spot. Bourbon + lemon + honey — three ingredients, now worldwide.*
- **Penicillin:** A soothing, fiery kick of whiskey, ginger, and honey, served with candied ginger.
  *Story: modern classic, 2005, Australian bartender Sam Ross at Milk & Honey NYC. A riff on the Gold Rush with a split scotch base (blend + peaty Islay float, first Compass Box Peat Monster) and honey-ginger syrup. Named for the medicine — a hot-toddy logic. Now IBA-official; Ross also wrote the Paper Plane (2007).*
- **Highlander Highball:** Tropical pineapple and tart lime shaken with robust whiskey and topped with bubbly soda.
  *Story: house-style modern variation — no classic history.*

- **Gin & Tonic:** Crisp, botanical London dry gin stirred with premium tonic and fresh lime.
  *Story: medicine first. British officers in 1800s India dosed bitter quinine (cinchona bark) against malaria, softened it with sugar, water, and gin; lime guarded against scurvy. Commercial tonic (Erasmus Bond 1858; Schweppes Indian Quinine Tonic 1870) made it a ritual. Churchill's line: it "saved more Englishmen's lives, and minds, than all the doctors in the Empire." First "gin and tonic" in print: an 1868 Lucknow horse-race report.*
- **Tom Collins:** A tall, breezy glass of crisp gin, sweet-tart lime, and bubbly club soda.
  *Story: London, early 1800s — headwaiter John Collin(s) of Limmer's Old House served a gin punch lengthened with soda. Named for Old Tom (sweeter) gin; first printed in Jerry Thomas's 1876 book. Bonus lore: the 1874 "Tom Collins Hoax," a New York prank sending men bar-hopping after a fictitious gossip named Tom Collins.*
- **Gin-Gin Mule:** Crisp gin and fresh mint muddled with sharp lime and spicy ginger syrup.
  *Story: modern classic, 2000, Audrey Saunders (created for Beacon, signature at Pegu Club NYC 2005–2020). Dale DeGroff taught her the Mojito in 1999 — her first fresh-herb cocktail — so she swapped rum for gin and added ginger beer. Built as a "gateway gin drink for vodka lovers"; her fresh-ginger infusion is "non-negotiable."*
- **Floradora:** Sweet muddled strawberries and crisp gin lifted by lime and a ginger-beer fizz.
  *Story: named for Florodora, the 1899 London / 1900 Broadway smash (552 shows) famous for its Sextette — six chorus girls who famously married millionaires. Legend: in 1901 dancer Susie Drake demanded "something brand new" from barman Jimmy O'Brien, who built her gin + raspberry + lime + ginger ale. In print by 1913 (Straub's, misspelled "Floradora" — the typo stuck). Likely poured at the Waldorf-Astoria.*
- **Eastside:** An ultra-cooling splash of crisp gin, fresh cucumber, muddled mint, and tart lime.
  *Story: a Southside with cucumber. The Southside (gin + mint + citrus) prints in 1917 (Ensslin; Chicago's South Side, the 21 Club, and Long Island's Southside club all claim it). The cucumbered Eastside is early-2000s NYC: George Delgado's 2004 long drink at Libation, served up at Milk & Honey (via Chad Solomon / Christy Pope). Hendrick's — rose and cucumber botanicals, launched 1999 — is its natural partner.*
- **Fitzgerald:** Botanical gin and fresh lemon brightened with a pinch of salt and aromatic bitters.
  *Story: modern classic, 1990s, Dale DeGroff at the Rainbow Room NYC. Improvised on the spot as the "Gin Thing" for a regular bored of G&Ts; a customer suggested naming it after F. Scott Fitzgerald (the menu already had a Hemingway Daiquiri, and Fitzgerald drank gin — supposedly because it couldn't be smelled on the breath). Printed in Craft of the Cocktail (2002). Unknowingly revives the 1922 Bennett, reprinted in the Savoy (1930).*
- **White Lady:** A velvety, cloud-like shake of crisp gin, orange liqueur, fresh lime, and egg white.
  *Story: Harry MacElhone, 1919, Ciro's Club London — originally with crème de menthe; reworked with gin at Harry's New York Bar Paris in 1929. Harry Craddock printed the gin version in the Savoy Cocktail Book (1930), and the Savoy claims it as his. A sidecar with gin instead of brandy; reportedly a Laurel & Hardy favorite. Also known as the Delilah / Chelsea Sidecar.*
- **Fine & Dandy:** A bone-dry, punchy sibling to the White Lady, seasoned with aromatic bitters.
  *Story: printed in the Savoy Cocktail Book (1930) — essentially a White Lady with bitters, "perfectly fitting any gentleman."*

- **Mojito:** Muddled lime and fresh mint layered with white rum for a crisp, minty breeze.
  *Story: Havana. Legend traces it to "El Draque" — the aguardiente + lime + mint + sugar ration of Francis Drake's crew, Cuba 1586, dosed against scurvy. When Bacardi's refined rums arrived in the 1800s, aguardiente was swapped out and the drink became the Mojito. First "Mojito" in print: Sloppy Joe's Bar manual, 1931–32. La Bodeguita del Medio (1942) claims it introduced the drink to Hemingway — including his famous wall graffiti, which biographers call a plant. The name likely comes from "mojo" (Cuban lime seasoning) or the African "mojo," to cast a spell.*
- **Pineapple Punch:** Lush pineapple, lime, and white rum shaken with mint and topped with ginger ale.
  *Story: no single origin — built on the 1878 Planter's Punch template (1 sour, 2 sweet, 3 strong, 4 weak; see draft-new-cocktails.md), tropical-fruit style.*
- **Cuba Libre:** A simple, timeless mix of white rum, fresh lime, and sweet, fizzy cola.
  *Story: Havana, August 1900. A US Army Signal Corps captain mixed Bacardi with Coca-Cola (brought over by Americans after the Spanish-American War) and lime at the American Bar, then toasted "¡Por Cuba libre!" — free Cuba. The battle cry stuck as the name. US troops spread it worldwide in WWII, helped by the Andrews Sisters' 1944 smash "Rum and Coca-Cola."*
- **Strawberry Rumble:** Muddled sweet strawberries and fresh lime on a light, lively white rum base.
  *Story: house original — no classic history.*
- **Company Buck:** Rich dark rum, sweet pineapple, and tart lime sparked with ginger and bitters.
  *Story: a rum buck. Bucks (spirit + ginger ale/beer + citrus) descend from the Horse's Neck — a non-alcoholic 1800s ginger-ale-and-lemon cooler; adding liquor gave the horse a "kick," hence the buck. Buck formulas print as early as Jerry Thomas's 1887 guide. The Moscow Mule is just the vodka buck that conquered America.*
- **Dark 'N' Stormy:** Moody dark rum layered over fiery ginger beer — drinks crisp and bites back gently.
  *Story: Bermuda's national drink. James Gosling was becalmed 91 days en route to Virginia in 1806, settled in Bermuda instead, and built a rum house; "Black Seal" comes from the black-wax-sealed reclaimed champagne bottles (Royal Navy officers' mess, WWI era). In the early 1920s the British officers' mess mixed Black Seal into homemade ginger beer from the naval club's factory — a sailor called it "the colour of a cloud only a fool or dead man would sail under." One of the only trademarked cocktails in the world: legally, only Gosling's Black Seal makes one.*
- **Dark 'N' Hoppy:** Deep dark rum, fiery ginger, and tart lime stretched out with a splash of lager.
  *Story: house variation on the Dark 'N' Stormy (above) — lager stretches the storm.*

- **Coco 'Cốm' Highball:** Nutty young rice-infused vodka paired with sweet coconut water and a tonic finish.
  *Story: house original — no classic history.*
- **Twisted Cosmopolitan:** A sweet strawberry twist on the classic, blending vodka, lime, and orange liqueur.
  *Story: the Cosmo is disputed three ways — Neal Murray's 1975 Minneapolis Kamikaze-plus-cranberry ("How cosmopolitan"); Cheryl Cook's 1985–86 South Beach "pretty in pink" (Absolut Citron + triple sec + Rose's lime + cranberry); and the canon version by Toby Cecchini (+ Melissa Huffsmith-Roth) at the Odeon NYC, 1988–89 (Citron + Cointreau + fresh lime + cranberry, pale, lemon twist). Dale DeGroff's 1996 flamed-orange version and Sex and the City season 2 made it global. Cecchini calls it his "albatross." Ours adds the strawberry twist.*
- **Watermelon Mint Cooler:** Juicy watermelon and slapped mint leaves shaken with vodka and topped with bubbly soda.
  *Story: house original — no classic history.*
- **Moscow Mule:** Crisp vodka kicked up with sharp lime, sweet-spicy ginger, and bubbly soda.
  *Story: Los Angeles/Hollywood, 1941 — "the drink that launched vodka in America." John G. Martin (Heublein, stuck with a Smirnoff distillery nobody wanted) met Jack Morgan (Cock 'n Bull on Sunset Strip, stuck with ginger beer nobody wanted); copper mugs came via Russian immigrant Sophie Berezinski and her 2,000 mugs. First mixed at the Chatham Hotel, then sold bar-to-bar with now-legendary Polaroid photos. Technically a vodka buck — the copper mug is the costume.*
- **Screwdriver:** Freshly squeezed orange juice completely enveloping a heavy pour of crisp vodka.
  *Story: WWII. Americans in China and Turkey mixed local neutral spirits with orange juice; the name surfaces in Ankara in 1943–44 (Newsweek: "interned American aviators") and a 1949 Time dispatch from Istanbul's Park Hotel. Legends credit Persian Gulf oil workers — or Detroit auto workers spiking breakfast OJ — stirring with an actual screwdriver for lack of a spoon.*
- **Caipiroska:** A rustic, sweet, and aggressively sharp crusher of vodka and muddled lime.
  *Story: the vodka passport of Brazil's caipirinha. The caipirinha (cachaça + lime + sugar, São Paulo interior ~1918, declared Brazilian cultural heritage in 2003) got a vodka twin as imported vodka spread through South America in the 1970s–80s — huge in Brazil, Uruguay, Argentina, Paraguay. With premium vodka, Brazilians call it "especial" or "luxo."*

- **Margarita:** Earthy tequila blanco and sharp lime with a savory, classic salted rim.
  *Story: famously disputed. Leading tale: Carlos "Danny" Herrera, Rancho La Gloria, Tijuana ~1938, built it for Ziegfeld girl Marjorie King — allergic to every spirit but tequila, unwilling to drink it straight. Rival: socialite Margarita Sames, Acapulco 1948. Historian David Wondrich traces it to the Brandy Daisy ("margarita" is Spanish for daisy); Jose Cuervo advertised "Margarita: it's more than a girl's name" as early as 1945.*
- **Tequila Sunrise:** Earthy tequila, sweet orange juice, and a deep red pool of sweet grenadine.
  *Story: two acts. The name starts at the Arizona Biltmore in the 1930s (Gene Sulit's tequila + crème de cassis + lime + soda). The modern red-gold layered drink is Bobby Lozoff + Billy Rice's, the Trident, Sausalito, early 70s — served to Mick Jagger at Bill Graham's June 1972 party kicking off the Stones tour, which the band dubbed the "cocaine and tequila sunrise tour." The Eagles' 1973 hit did the rest; Cuervo printed the recipe on bottles. Sausalito placed a historical marker in 2023; Lozoff died in 2025.*
- **Tequila Gimlet:** Agave-forward tequila blanco balanced with sharp lime and simple syrup.
  *Story: a gimlet with tequila swapped in. The gimlet was Royal Navy scurvy medicine — daily lime rations made drinkable with gin (possibly at Rear-Admiral Gimlette's suggestion; possibly named for the shipboard tool), built on Lauchlan Rose's 1867 lime cordial. First printed by Harry MacElhone in 1923 (half Plymouth, half Rose's); Raymond Chandler's The Long Goodbye (1953) made it literary: "half gin and half Rose's… beats Martinis hollow." Hemingway carried it on safari; Betty Draper drank it on Mad Men.*
- **Mexican Mule:** Earthy tequila blanco locking paws with sharp lime, spicy ginger, and bubbly soda.
  *Story: a Moscow Mule with tequila — see the Moscow Mule story above (1941, Martin + Morgan + copper mugs).*
- **Ranch Water:** A deeply hydrating highball of crisp tequila, sharp lime, and a savory pinch of salt.
  *Story: Texas, late 90s. Kevin Williamson mixed tequila and lime in his water bottle on hunting trips with his father, then menued it at Ranch 616, Austin (opened 1998; on the menu by 1999): reposado + orange liqueur + lime with Topo Chico on the side. He carried it to the Gage Hotel, Marathon, in 2010; the Washington Post credited him as originator in 2021. Williamson died later that year; the canned imitators came after. Purists: Topo Chico or it isn't one.*
