## 1. Bee's Knees

- **Category guess:** Cocktails / Gin-based
- **Price guess:** VND 139k
- **Tags:** Citrus, Floral, Smooth
- **Description draft:** Gin with honey, lemon, and a touch of orange. Soft, round, easy to drink.
- **Story behind:** Prohibition-era America (1920s). The name was flapper slang for "the best" — same family as "the cat's pajamas." Honey and lemon existed to mask the harsh smell and taste of bathtub gin. No confirmed inventor: candidates include Ritz Paris bartender Frank Meier and "Unsinkable" Molly Brown; the recipe reached print by the early 1930s. (Some versions add orange juice, like ours.)
- **Recipe:**
  - Gin 45ml (1.5 oz)
  - Lemon juice 20ml
  - Honey syrup 10ml
  - Simple syrup 10ml
  - Orange juice 10ml
- **Serve:** Shake, coupe or rock glass. Garnish: lemon peel.
- **Status:** bones

## 2. Pink Ocean

- **Category guess:** Signature
- **Price guess:** VND 159k

- **Description draft:** Strawberry soda base with a blue rum float on top. Sweet, cold, looks like sunset over water.

## 4. Orange Blossom

- **Category guess:** Signature
- **Price guess:** VND 149k
- **Tags:** Citrus, Sweet, Tall
- **Description draft:** Gin, orange, and grenadine over ice. Tall, juicy, slightly sweet with a lime edge.
- **Story behind:** A real Prohibition-era classic. Like the Bee's Knees, it was built to make bathtub gin drinkable — ginargo mixed with orange juice (the original also used sweet vermouth). Ours swaps the vermouth for triple sec, lime, and grenadine — same spirit, brighter build.
- **Recipe:**
  - Tulip glass, fill with ice
  - Gin 30ml
  - Triple sec 15ml
  - Lime juice 15ml
  - Grenadine 15ml — stir
  - Top up: orange juice 60ml
- **Serve:** Tulip glass, full ice.
- **Ref:** https://www.instagram.com/reel/Db0w8svNb9J/?stkn=dXNsMzNyN3VoZmVy
- **Status:** bones — confirm gin amount vs sweetness

## 6. Daiquiri

- **Category guess:** Cocktails / Rum-based
- **Price guess:** VND 129k
- **Tags:** Sour, Citrus, Clean
- **Description draft:** White rum, lime, and sugar. Nothing extra — tart, clean, strong.
- **Story behind:** Cuba, 1898. American mining engineer Jennings Stockton Cox ran out of gin while entertaining guests at the Daiquirí iron mine near Santiago — so he reached for local rum, lime, and sugar over ice and named it after the town. A US Navy officer carried it to the Army and Navy Club in Washington DC in 1909; Havana's El Floridita ("La Cuna del Daiquiri," the cradle of the daiquiri) perfected it under bartender Constante Ribalaigua. Later devotees: Hemingway and JFK.
- **Recipe:**
  - Rum 45ml
  - Lime juice 25ml
  - Simple syrup 15ml
- **Serve:** Shake, coupe or rock glass.
- **Status:** bones — classic, ready to promote when wanted

## 8. Planter's Punch

- **Category guess:** Cocktails / Rum-based
- **Price guess:** VND 139k
- **Tags:** Fruity, Spiced, Sour
- **Description draft:** Dark rum with lime, grenadine, and bitters. Deep, fruity, a little bitter on the end.
- **Story behind:** Jamaica. The first printed recipe appeared in September 1878 in Fun, a London humor magazine — written in verse as "A West Indian Recipe": one of sour, two of sweet, three of strong, four of weak ("at least, so they say in Jamaica"). A rival claim credits the Planter's Hotel in Charleston, but no early source backs it. The IBA spec strips it to Jamaican rum, lime, and cane sugar; grenadine and bitters (like ours) are the standard modern build.
- **Recipe:**
  - Dark rum 30ml
  - Lime juice 30ml
  - Simple syrup 15ml
  - Grenadine 15ml
  - Bitters 3 dashes
- **Serve:** Shake, highball with full small ice.
- **Status:** bones — classic, ready to promote when wanted

## 9. Hot Buttered Whiskey

- **Category guess:** Signature
- **Price guess:** VND 139k
- **Tags:** Warm, Spiced, Rich
- **Description draft:** Hot bourbon with melted butter, cinnamon, and sugar. Warm, rich, winter in a glass — our only hot serve.
- **Story behind:** The hot buttered rum goes back to Colonial America — New England colonists mulling rum with butter, sugar, and spice (butter in hot drinks traces to Henry VIII's England). Jerry Thomas printed it in the 1860s; novelist Kenneth Roberts revived it in 1937's Northwest Passage. Ours swaps rum for bourbon — a modern variation on a 300-year-old warmer.
- **Recipe:**
  - Whiskey (bourbon) 45ml
  - Unsalted butter 1 tablespoon (~15ml)
  - Simple syrup 15ml
  - Cinnamon syrup 10ml
  - Hot water (not quite boiling) 75ml
- **Serve:** Pour one by one, stir to dissolve. Rock glass or coupe. Garnish: whipped cream (preferably) / cinnamon stick.
- **Status:** bones — test in Saigon heat; a hot drink is a bold call, confirm demand first

## 10. Painkiller

- **Category guess:** Signature
- **Price guess:** VND 159k
- **Tags:** Tropical, Creamy, Rich
- **Description draft:** Dark rum, pineapple, orange, and coconut with fresh nutmeg. Thick, sweet, dangerous — the tiki heavyweight.
- **Story behind:** White Bay, Jost Van Dyke, British Virgin Islands, 1970s. George and Marie Myrick perfected it at the Sandcastle Hotel; second owner Daphne Henderson renamed the bar the Soggy Dollar (no dock — you swim ashore, dollars get soggy). Named after a local cure-all bush. Pusser's founder Charles Tobias recreated it (4-1-1: pineapple, orange, coconut), won a blind tasting 8–0, and trademarked it with her blessing. ⚠️ The name "Painkiller" is a Pusser's trademark — consider a house name before going live.
- **Recipe:**
  - Dark rum 45ml
  - Pineapple juice 45ml
  - Orange juice 30ml
  - Coconut milk / cream of coconut 30ml
- **Serve:** Shake, tiki glass. Garnish: orange wheel + grated nutmeg.
- **Status:** bones — decide on rename (trademark); confirm coconut milk vs cream of coconut

## 17. Moka Martini (Espresso Martini variation)

- **Category guess:** Signature
- **Price guess:** VND 149k
- **Tags:** Coffee, Rich, Smooth
- **Description draft:** Vodka, coffee liqueur, and real moka-pot coffee, shaken to a foam. Bitter, smooth, awake — our take on the modern classic.
- **Story behind:** The Espresso Martini is Dick Bradsell's, Soho Brasserie London 1983 — built for a model who asked for something to "wake me up and fuck me up." Born as the Vodka Espresso (on the rocks, no liqueur), renamed through the 90s (including a stint as the Pharmaceutical Stimulant), finished with the coupe glass and three-bean garnish. Ours swaps espresso-machine shots for moka-pot coffee — same idea, deeper roast.
- **Recipe:**
  - Vodka 30ml
  - Kahlúa (coffee liqueur) 15ml
  - Simple syrup 5ml
  - Moka-pot coffee 20ml
- **Serve:** Reverse dry shake, coupe or martini glass. Garnish: cocoa powder.
- **Status:** bones — test foam from moka coffee vs espresso; confirm "Moka" vs "Espresso" naming on menu

## 18. Black Russian

- **Category guess:** Cocktails / Vodka-based
- **Price guess:** VND 129k
- **Tags:** Coffee, Rich, Spirit-Forward
- **Description draft:** Vodka and coffee liqueur over big ice. Dark, bitter, two ingredients — nothing to hide behind.
